# Roadmaps and member plans

Read this procedure when creating/revising a roadmap, coordinating its execution or completion, or working with a roadmap-owned plan. Standalone task plans need no roadmap. [Plan lifecycle](plan-lifecycle.md) owns the shared names, locations, lookup and independent state transitions; [planning](planning.md) owns plan contents and readiness.

## Purpose and contents

Use a roadmap for a common outcome that needs several independently planned tasks. An ordinary plan with stages is sufficient for a single task; creating a roadmap is not a mandatory planning step.

In addition to the applicable plan contents, record:

- The overall goal, boundaries and observable completion criteria.
- Full plan-name references in the intended order, with the reason for consequential dependencies. A numbered list is enough for a sequence; describe parallel or conditional work only where needed.
- Conditions needed before the next plan can proceed, and any outstanding decisions affecting that order or the overall outcome.

Resolve existing plan references against their originals. Distinguish them from proposed work whose plan has not yet been authored; identify that gap without claiming a file exists or creating task plans outside the authorized planning scope. A missing expected plan remains a missing-reference problem under the lifecycle procedure, not permission to reconstruct it.

Keep detailed scope, steps, criteria and evidence in each task plan. The roadmap owns cross-plan order and aggregate criteria, not duplicate task contents or a competing table of task statuses. Read the relevant plans when their actual progress or outcomes matter.

## Ownership and references

A member has one physical owning roadmap, identified by its grouping directory in the standard store. Resolve that owner's file by its full name across the states; it need not be alongside the member. Read its goal, ordering and applicable constraints before working on the member. A roadmap name alone is not evidence that its file exists or that it contains the expected relationship; surface a missing or conflicting owner/context instead of guessing.

A roadmap can also refer to an existing standalone plan or a plan owned elsewhere. Keep the single original at its established location; additional references do not adopt, move, rename or copy it. Distinguish owned members from referenced dependencies so references do not silently create multiple owners. Use explicit owner/reference bindings where a stronger nonstandard destination applies.

Roadmaps reference task plans, not nested roadmaps. Keep one grouping level rather than adding a recursive plan tree.

## Execute or resume

1. Read the roadmap and the relevant original plans. Recover the currently agreed scope, material decisions and evidence using the main skill's start/resume procedure.
2. Determine the next permitted action from the assignment, order, dependencies and current outcomes. An archived plan may be cancelled rather than completed; directory placement alone cannot establish readiness. Report a blocked or unresolved prerequisite before affected execution.
3. Carry out only authorized work. A roadmap-preparation request ends with planning; selection as focused does not authorize the entire sequence. An explicit assignment to execute a roadmap may cover successive plans without an artificial approval request before each, subject to existing scope/decision gates and actual prerequisites.
4. Update the document that owns each changed fact. Apply lifecycle changes to the selected documents individually; preserve the identities, independent states and material bindings of other plans.

Following a roadmap relationship is contextual reading, not permission to change its state or edit every listed plan. If an individual assignment conflicts with the roadmap's order or constraints, resolve the consequential difference under the global decision rules instead of silently ignoring either. Read-only investigation or planning may proceed within its actual authorization while an implementation prerequisite remains unresolved.

## Completion, pause and cancellation

Assess a roadmap against its own overall criteria and relevant task evidence. All referenced plans being archived is insufficient: some may be cancelled, and individually completed tasks may not establish the aggregate result. Preserve unmet criteria and required acceptance rather than declaring success from file counts or directory states.

The common lifecycle applies to each document independently. Pausing, completing or cancelling a roadmap does not by itself move or cancel its members; changing one member does not close its roadmap. Record the scope of explicit collective decisions and each affected outcome. A paused or archived owner remains context, not a fresh grant to execute its remaining plans.
