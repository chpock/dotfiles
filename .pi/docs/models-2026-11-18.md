# Model assessment — 2026-11-18

**Scope:** A focused refresh for GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol, compared with GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and the retained GPT-6 Luna reference. Sources were inspected on **2026-11-18**. OpenAI API contracts, Codex ChatGPT-sign-in access and included subscription accounting are distinct. This report does not establish individual account entitlement or local model performance.

The [selection guide](model-selection.md) owns the task tiers, capability profiles and research method. The [2026-10-17 assessment](models-2026-10-17.md) remains dated evidence for the earlier generation and broader historical comparison. Configuration choices belong in [settings.md](settings.md), [pi-subagents.md](pi-subagents.md) and the [decision journal](model-decisions.md).

## Findings

- **GPT-6.2 Sol** is the best-supported successor to Sol 6.1 for General-capability, multistep technical work: current task guidance explicitly includes coding, planning from several sources and iterative testing/revision; published Codex Standard token rates are unchanged [N1, N4–N6].
- **GPT-6.2 Astra** is the best-supported successor to Astra 6 for demanding, completeness-sensitive work. OpenAI reports improved professional-work results; its published Standard Codex input/output rates are 20% lower and cached-input rates 60% lower than Astra 6 [N1, N4–N6]. These are per-token comparisons, not whole-task or subscription savings guarantees.
- The evidence does **not** establish Sol/Astra equivalence, code-review recall, research factuality at a particular effort, or that one effort label means the same depth across generations. Keep model capability separate from reasoning effort and task authority [N1, N5–N7].
- **Luna remains a distinct focused/well-scoped option**, not displaced merely because the larger families have successors. Current guidance still assigns it clear, repeatable tasks and its token weights remain much lighter [N4–N6].

## Model interface and reasoning support

Both 6.2 API model pages declare text/image input, text output, **1,050,000 context**, **922,000 maximum input** and **128,000 maximum output** [N2, N3]. Those are provider API limits, not an instruction to enlarge a client's context budget.

Both reject reasoning effort `none`. The migration guide recommends `low` instead of an existing `none`/`minimal` setting and `medium` as a starting point for a new integration. Official selection guidance includes Sol Extra High and Astra Medium/Extra High; the release's FrontierCode comparison explicitly uses **max for both models** [N1, N6, N7]. The inspected model pages do not establish a universal API default or a calibrated correspondence between generations' effort labels. Client mappings and selected fixed efforts belong in the configuration guides.

Tool calling requires **Responses**, even though Chat Completions is supported. The migration guide also lists unsupported sampling/log-probability parameters and separate cache-retention controls [N7, N8]. Do not infer endpoint compatibility from a matching bare model ID.

Starting with GPT-6.2, OpenAI says history-prefix edits, a model downgrade, or a change of end user/organization can cause reasoning items to be dropped. That can reduce answer quality or require repeated work. Prefix stability and retained reasoning are therefore relevant to total-work efficiency; a model-name change alone does not establish how a particular client/context manager preserves them [N7].

## Included subscription consumption

The current Codex pricing page estimates local messages per five hours for **Plus and Standard Business**, not a fixed message limit [N4]:

| Model | Estimated local-message range |
| --- | ---: |
| Astra 6.2 | 6–50 |
| Astra 6 | 5–45 |
| Sol 6.2 | 17–180 |
| Sol 6.1 | 15–160 |
| Luna 6 | 350–3,000 |

Pro currently has no five-hour limit according to that page; weekly/other limits and shared product usage still matter. These overlapping estimates do not establish matched-task depletion ratios or an individual account's capacity.

The page explicitly states that credit prices alone do not determine included subscription usage. It separately exempts Sol 6.1 subscription usage from a long-context surcharge; that exemption must not be silently transferred to Sol 6.2. The exact new-model long-context included-allowance conversion is not established by the inspected material [N4]. Long-context Sol 6.2 usage could therefore consume more included allowance than Sol 6.1 despite unchanged Standard token rates if that exemption does not extend. This is an unquantified possibility, not confirmation of a surcharge; the API's 272,000-input-token pricing threshold does not establish a subscription threshold.

## Relative economic weights

**Reference: GPT-6.2 Sol = 1 per token category, Codex Standard credits, accessed 2026-11-18.** Sol 6.1 has identical published rates, so this reference preserves continuity with the earlier report. These weights are not included-quota multipliers [N4].

| Model | Uncached input | Cached input | Output |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Sol 6.2 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Sol 6.1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Astra 6.2 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Astra 6 | 5 | 10 | 5 |
| Luna 6 | 0.05 | 0.10 | 0.05 |

Raw published credits per million `(uncached, cached, output)` are Sol 6.2 and 6.1 **`(50, 2.5, 250)`**, Astra 6.2 **`(200, 10, 1000)`**, Astra 6 **`(250, 25, 1250)`**, and Luna 6 **`(2.5, 0.25, 12.5)`** [N4]. Thus Astra 6.2 / Astra 6 is `(0.8, 0.4, 0.8)`.

For an illustrative **100,000 uncached input tokens, no cached input and 10,000 output tokens**, the published rate-card totals are 7.5 credits for either Sol, 30 for Astra 6.2, 37.5 for Astra 6 and 0.375 for Luna. This holds tokens fixed; it is not a representative workload measurement or a subscription forecast.

API rate comparisons support the same directional finding, but remain a separate accounting path. Both 6.2 API pages apply higher full-request rates above **272,000 input tokens**: input/cache rates double and output is 1.5 times the short-context rate. API cache writes and speed tiers have separate charges. Codex credit billing states there is no separate cache-write charge [N2–N4]. No change to speed tier is implied by choosing a successor.

## Task-specific capabilities

| Evidence | Reported result or recommendation | Interpretation and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official task-selection guide [N6] | Sol 6.2 Medium for existing-codebase bug fixes/features and drafts; Extra High for multistep work with testing/revision. Astra Medium for complex projects and Extra High for open-ended trade-offs. | Direct task-fit guidance, not a requirement to reset every existing integration to medium or increase every Astra task to Extra High. |
| Release: GDPval v2 [N1] | Astra 6.2 improves **6.2 percentage points** over Astra 6 while using **22% fewer output tokens**; Sol 6.2 improves **5.3 points** over Sol 6.1. | Provider-reported long-horizon professional-work results. The Astra token reduction is not a universal Sol reduction or a quota multiplier. |
| Release: FrontierCode 2.0 [N1] | Sol 6.2 comes within **0.5 points** of Astra 6.2 at about **one-fifth the cost**, both at max. | Code-change acceptance benchmark, not review recall, matched high-vs-high advice quality or proof of interchangeable models. Cost per evaluated task differs from rate-card weights. |
| Release: Agents' Last Exam [N1] | Astra 6.2 is **5.2 points** ahead of Astra 6 at less than one-third the reported cost. | Provider-reported professional workflow evaluation; no local reproduction or universal cost reduction is established. |
| Current product guidance [N5] | Astra for sustained reasoning across code/apps/research; Sol for everyday coding and multi-source work; Luna for clear, repeatable tasks. | Supports retaining distinct task tiers, not automatic migration of every executor to the largest model. |

The release also reports two alignment evaluations in which the new models were more likely to omit obstacles such as missing files or unavailable search tools. OpenAI says Codex-specific instructions substantially reduced those omissions; this is not evidence that another harness receives that mitigation. It also reports tighter cyber safeguards that can restrict legitimate security research [N1]. Capability improvements do not remove the need to verify evidence, failures and completion.

### Capability-profile mapping

Definitions remain in the [selection guide](model-selection.md#capability-and-reasoning-profiles).

| Profile | Candidate and fit | Confidence and gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focused | Luna 6 for narrow extraction, navigation and repeatable work [N5, N6]. | Medium-high task guidance; no new comparison of local executor performance. |
| General | Sol 6.2 for complex, revisable work, with Medium through Extra High guidance depending on task shape [N1, N5, N6]. | Medium-high task-fit support; no measured transfer of old-generation effort thresholds. |
| Deep | Astra 6.2 for demanding analysis, conflicting evidence and completeness-sensitive work [N1, N5, N6]. | Medium: strong provider-reported capability evidence, but no direct audit/review-recall study for the local role prompts. |

The evidence supports moving within each larger model's existing task class, not promoting bounded execution to Astra, claiming Sol/max equals Astra/high, or relaxing verification. Preserve the distinction between published measurements, author recommendations and local selection judgments.
